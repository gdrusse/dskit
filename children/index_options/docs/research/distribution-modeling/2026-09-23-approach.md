## Question

How should the predictive component model where one index settles relative to
a condor's four strikes: full latent distribution, fixed bins, or a
threshold classifier?

## Finding

- **Coordinates:** express every threshold in volatility-standardized
  log-moneyness, z = ln(K/F) / (sigma_hat * sqrt(tau)). Listed strikes step
  in fixed points, so dollar/percent regions drift with level and vol; in
  z-units the strike regions (shorts ~0.5-1.5 SD, wings to ~2.5 SD) recur.
- **Primary model:** a continuous conditional distribution of z_T under
  the physical (real-world) measure, fit on history (e.g. GJR/HAR-GARCH +
  filtered historical simulation, or skewed-t / NGBoost). Integrate it over
  each candidate's strike regions; any strikes, width, or DTE, no crossing.
- **Challenger:** distribution regression P(z_T <= k | x) on a k-grid in
  z-units (stacked logits, monotone rearrangement). Kept only if it beats
  the primary on region-weighted scores.
- **Dropped:** fixed bins in points (non-stationary relative to the
  distribution; cannot score arbitrary strikes).
- **Option-implied info:** IV / variance-risk-premium as features or a
  mixture component, never the raw risk-neutral density as a real-world
  forecast (it overstates tails).
- **Support:** academic-led. Industry supports only the vol-scaled strike
  convention (delta / expected-move). No published head-to-head of full
  density vs region-focused modeling for condor decisions was found.

## Question

Can repeated chronological validation select a Lasso feature count empirically and produce a stable improvement over the original ten-feature model, without a one-standard-error rule?

## Finding

The authorized procedure rebuilt full-Lasso rankings inside each training fold, using coefficients alone. For forecast year Y, the three validation periods were Q4(Y-2), Q1(Y-1) and Q2(Y-1), with expanding training and settlement purges. Source ranking penalty and subset refit penalty each used the original seven-alpha grid; all counts 1–106 plus the null forecast were included. Each annual choice minimized pooled row-weighted exact projected-CDF 13-node twCRPS over 5,195 settings, with no one-SE preference. A fixed-ten/null control independently tuned the same two penalties under identical rolling validation. The original historical-monitor ten-feature models were copied unchanged as a second control. The original monitor was check-only for both new procedures. All five primary and five rolling fixed-ten models were frozen before monitor and annual scoring. This study does not assume that ten must be optimal.

Across 2020–2023, the rolling procedure selects 4, 19, 4 and 3 inputs; all remain active after refitting. It gains 0.305% versus calibrated market, compared with 0.204% for ten features under the same rolling procedure and 0.471% for the original ten-feature Lasso. Allowing the count to vary improves the point estimate versus its matched fixed-ten control by 0.101% (95% paired interval −0.102% to +0.305%), but trails the original ten by 0.166% (−0.423% to +0.090%). Neither direct comparison is conclusive. The new model’s gain over calibrated market also has an interval spanning zero: −0.122% to +0.732%.

This is an executable selection rule, not evidence of convergence to ten or a superior subset. Omitting one validation quarter produces counts 10/4/4, 19/72/3, 45/1/1 and 3/21/21. The 2021 fit gains 3.318% over the constant in training but loses 0.658% on the later historical check, a warning about overfitting or changes in market conditions. The other three fits retain positive check gains. Changing validation also worsens the fixed-ten control versus the original by 0.268% (pointwise interval −0.448% to −0.088%); count alone does not explain the result. Keep the original ten as the current benchmark, without treating ten as an established optimum. The run is complete; it does not establish the desired stable, better method.

Primary twCRPS is 0.527617663028 for the empirical count, 0.528151939740 for rolling fixed ten, and 0.526740719780 for original ten. Full CRPS is respectively 0.663085883129, 0.663667943622 and 0.662117301284. All models use the same 449,498 identities in 2020–2023. Annual primary gains over calibrated market are -0.150495%, +0.160503%, +0.909436% and +0.269645% for the empirical method. The corresponding original-ten gains are +0.567292%, +0.728471%, +0.333309% and +0.255432%. New selected ranking/refit penalties are (0.001,0.001), (0.001,0.001), (0.03,0.003) and (0.01,0.0001). The 2019 warmup selects 59 allowed and is excluded from performance ranking. Feature lists and count curves are retained in model/selection outputs.

Leaving out one inner quarter is sensitivity analysis only; alternatives do not change frozen annual fits and are not chronological forecasts because retained quarters may follow the omitted quarter. The average overlap between training-derived inner feature sets is 100%, 75.5%, 73.3% and 100% across the four annual choices. A stable ranking conditional on count does not imply a stable selected count. Training/check gaps can reflect both fitted noise and changes in market conditions; these results cannot establish absence of overfitting.

Synthetic checks proved validation labels cannot alter training-derived rankings and reproduced direct sklearn Lasso scores. Selection checks ensure a strictly better score wins without a one-SE preference, fixed-ten uses the same grid, null can win, and exact ties follow the registered enumeration. Verification independently reconstructs selected inner scores within 1.11e-16, confirms all pooled minima and label purges, and matches every reference/control score exactly. Annual float32 CDF rescoring agrees within 1.63e-7; source/final coefficient optimality residual is at most 4.40e-7, below the registered 2e-6 threshold. Some solver warning counts are retained in worker receipts; original-statistics optimality checks pass. All forecasts are finite, bounded and monotone. Source/model/input hashes and parent-study immutability pass. No full test suite was run.

All fifteen main inner jobs ran in WSL2 using four independent workers with two numerical threads each. Main jobs took approximately 114–194 seconds each and peaked at 275 MiB RSS; annual evaluation peaked at 3.38 GiB. A Mac pilot matched scores and rankings but differed in a few near-zero active counts; it was retained for audit and excluded from main results. The resource-only placement amendment replaced the proposed two WSL/two Mac setup with four WSL workers without changing the scientific method or relaxing parity criteria.

All 2020–2023 annual outcomes and the original monitor had already been inspected in previous studies. These are retrospective development results with pointwise intervals, not untouched confirmation or intervals adjusted for repeated model searches. Historical feature source-time concerns, including open-interest fields, remain unresolved. Protected 2024–2025 and 2026 stayed sealed. No MIO, allocation or production promotion was performed.

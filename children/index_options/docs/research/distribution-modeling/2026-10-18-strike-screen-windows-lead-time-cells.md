### 2. Lead-time choice rule (written before outcomes; applied to the 2017-2019 census)

**2.1 Definitions.** For symbol s and quote day t, a listed expiry E is two-sided valid when it has >= 1 call and >= 1 put with bid > 0 and ask >= bid; a lead-time rule R maps (s, t) to at most one E (the nearest expiry matching R's cycle and DTE range); coverage c(s, y; R) = share of s's quote days in year y with a matching E. A quote day is resolvable for R when the matched E has >= 4 two-sided strikes (two strikes on either side of the forward can resolve the inner 0.5-z bins only when the strike step is <= 0.5 sigma; feature record 0 and 2.4).

**2.2 Admission rule.** R is a UNIVERSAL lead time when, in EACH development year 2017, 2018 and 2019, the median symbol has c >= 0.95 (X) and the p25 symbol has c >= 0.90 (Y). R is a SUBSET lead time when it fails Y but meets X in each year; its cohort is the set of names with c >= 0.90 in each of the three years, frozen in the registration. 2016 is outside the rule and outside the pilot (owner decision: 79 of 118 names unquoted before 2017-01-03). Reasons for X and Y: the census ceiling is 0.996 (one or two data-gap days per name-year), so 0.95 means "every day but gaps"; Y = 0.90 at the p25 guarantees that at least three quarters of the cohort miss <= 10 % of their identities, so the yearly replication of 1.7 compares the same names in 2020 and in 2023 rather than a weekly-listing composition drift (the 2019 p25 jump from 0.23 to 0.80 is exactly such a drift inside development); a lower Y would admit the fixed 7-day buckets with a cohort that changes by regime, and the cell counts of section 3 would then not be comparable across years. The rule is applied once, to the census, before any outcome column exists.

**2.3 Result.** No fixed 7-day DTE bucket passes (p25 0.22-0.38 for 7-44 DTE in 2017-2018; 0.48-0.50 for 45-59; 0.34-0.41 for 60-119; 120-269 fail X at 0.73-0.84). M1 (front standard monthly, DTE 15..49) passes as universal in all three years (p25 1.0, median 1.0; 112/118 names >= 0.90; the six below keep their covered days as identities and their uncovered days are counted exclusions identical for every model). W28 (any expiry, DTE 28..34) is a subset lead time with 74 names. M2, Q and the long buckets fail; W7 would qualify as a subset (71 names) but is not carried: its valid-quote share is the lowest (0.65 at 7-13 DTE) and a 3-sigma bin at 10 days has almost no realized support per year. Recommendation for the first screen: TWO lead times, M1 (universal, the monthly cycle, mean DTE ≈ 32, 118 names) and W28 (liquid subset, 74 names); nothing at 60-90 days (coverage 0.45-0.63, and the monitor band would be empty under `cal_n` 40). A third lead time is a promotion-rung question, not a screen question, and under 1.3 it needs `cal_n` ≈ 110 for Q.

**2.4 Target keyed to listed expiries.** Identity (symbol, quote_date, expiry = E(R)); settlement_date = last XNYS session on or before E (cdf_study 3446-3449; a holiday-Friday expiry becomes Thursday and matches); terminal_return = log(close_settlement / close_quote) on the split-adjusted close; actual_calendar_dte = settlement − quote_date (3451); sessions_to_expiry = planned sessions from quote_date to the planned settlement (3471); reference_scale = max(rv_22, 0.001) · sqrt(sessions_to_expiry) per row (3528): a 15-DTE M1 row and a 49-DTE row of the same name on the same day have reference scales in ratio sqrt(10/34), so z is comparable across DTE by construction and the same Z grid serves every lead time. `exact_dte` equality (2876) must become a lead-time selector (gap G-W1); `PriceCalendarCDFPanel` cannot express it. Corporate-action exclusion and the unadjusted-basis rule of the market record 4 apply per row.

**2.5 Sharing a model across lead times.** Within M1 the DTE varies continuously 15..49, so the model MUST condition on DTE through the existing continuous features `calendar_dte, sessions_to_expiry, log_calendar_dte, log_sessions_to_expiry, calendar_days_per_session, reference_scale` (template features 0-5); no one-hot inside a lead time. Across lead times the screen fits SEPARATE models per lead time (separate panels, documents and fold tables): the purge, the monitor band and the empirical baseline differ, and on a weekly name the W28 identity coincides with the M1 identity about one week in four (same expiry), which the identity pairing (`check_pairing`, cdf_experiment 279-292) refuses as a duplicate in one panel. A pooled M1 ∪ W28 arm, de-duplicated by identity with a `lead_time_id` one-hot added to the context features, is ONE extra promotion-rung cell, scored per lead time against the separate fits; it is the test of whether the shared DTE geometry transfers. The `horizon_empirical` baseline must condition on `lead_time_id` (or a coarse DTE bucket column), not on the exact DTE (`condition_indices`, predictive_cdf 1286-1288), otherwise 35 keys per symbol split about 750 fit rows into 20-row quantile sets.

**2.6 Multi-lead-time overlap in purging.** Purging is per row, so a 49-day label and a 31-day label of the same origin fall on the correct side of every boundary independently; the only cross-lead effect is that a longer lead time loses more origins at each boundary (1.2-1.3). In a pooled panel the boundary check (cdf_experiment 313-315) runs on the union, which is correct but stricter: the pooled monitor band is the intersection of the per-lead bands. A 90-day lead time would cross the yearly boundaries of (b) with about a quarter of each year's rows purged from the following train band; that is a reason, with coverage, not to carry it.

### 3. Mispricing cells, as statements about probabilities

**3.1 Cell.** c = (lead time L, strike region R, context K). L ∈ {M1, W28}. R ∈ the 14 regions of Z: the 12 inner bins [k, k + 0.5) for k = −3, −2.5, ..., 2.5 and the two tails (−∞, −3), [3, ∞); the region's event is 1{z_i ∈ R}. K ∈ {none} in the primary family; the secondary family adds the terciles of ONE context, `iv_h_over_rv` (feature record rank 1; cutoffs frozen on the fold's fit band, `ConditionalScorePockets` semantics), with `half_spread_over_premium` terciles as a second secondary context. Families, declared now: primary P = M1 × 14 regions × {none} = 14 cells; secondary S1 = W28 × 14 = 14; S2 = 2 × 14 × 3 (IV context) = 84; S3 = 2 × 14 × 3 (spread context) = 84; total 196 declared, all reported, all written to the `AttemptRegistry` with their family tag. No cell is added, merged or re-cut after an outcome is read. Put-side and call-side OTM bands are UNIONS of these regions (e.g. [−3, −1) and [1, 3)) and are reported as derived rows, not as new cells.

**3.2 Pre-registered quantities per cell (per lead time, per validation year and pooled).**
- Mass: m_X(c) = mean_i [F_X,i(b) − F_X,i(a)] for X ∈ {market_raw (Q), market_calibrated (Q transported on development), model, price_only, horizon_empirical}; tails use F(−3) and 1 − F(3).
- Realized frequency f(c) = mean_i 1{z_i ∈ R}; realized event count n_c = Σ_i 1{z_i ∈ R}.
- Calibration gap g_X(c) = m_X(c) − f(c) (calibration-in-the-large for the region).
- Region Brier B_X(c) = mean_i (p_X,i(c) − 1{z_i ∈ R})², p_X,i(c) the row's mass in R: a proper score for the region event, with the Murphy decomposition reliability − resolution + uncertainty reported ([Murphy 1973](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0450(1973)012<0595:ANVPOT>2.0.CO;2)); threshold Brier at the region's edges (F_X(k) − 1{z <= k})² for k ∈ {a, b} as the existing `decision_strike_brier` quantity; reliability per edge threshold by the CORP (isotonic) reliability diagram with its consistency band ([Dimitriadis, Gneiting & Jordan 2021](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2016191118)).
- Binned log score per lead time (not per cell): S_log,X = mean_i −log p_X,i(R(z_i)) over the 14-region partition; proper for the discretized law ([Gneiting & Raftery 2007](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)); the per-cell contribution is reported descriptively.
- Paired differences Δ_{X,Y}(c) = mean_i [B_X,i(c) − B_Y,i(c)] on identical identities, with the interval of 3.4; the threshold-weighted CRPS on the region ([Gneiting & Ranjan 2011](https://doi.org/10.1198/jbes.2010.08110)) is the existing `tail_crps` quantity when R is a tail interval.
- Support: rows, distinct quote dates, distinct expiries, symbols, n_c; the resolvable-subset versions of every quantity for |k| >= 1.5.

**3.3 What answers which question.** (Q-a) "Is the market's raw mass in c different from the realized frequency?" is g_Q(c) against zero: a one-sample calibration statement about a risk-neutral object scored as a physical forecast. It is expected to be non-zero and says nothing about any model. Its known causes are (i) the pricing kernel: the variance premium makes Q wider than P and the tail premium makes the left tail heavier ([Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhp008); [Bollerslev & Todorov 2011](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2011.01695.x); [Jackwerth 2000](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/13.2.433); [Aït-Sahalia & Lo 2000](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-4076(99)00016-0)); (ii) American exercise and borrow on single-stock options, which bias the mid-based parity forward and the put side ([Barone-Adesi & Whaley 1987](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1987.tb02569.x)); (iii) microstructure: mids of wide or stale quotes, zero bids in the wings (so the tail regions are the completion model, not the market), strike discreteness (a 0.5-z bin interpolated from strikes 0.5-2 sigma apart), the unadjusted basis. (Q-b) "Is the gap a stationary premium?" is g_QP(c) on validation with the transport fitted on development: near zero means a constant monotone map removes it; a non-zero g_QP that replicates across the four years is the only thing this note allows to be called "a systematically miscalibrated cell of the transported market", and it is still a statement about probabilities with causes (i)-(iii) listed as candidates, never about value. (Q-c) "Does the model carry information beyond the stationary-transported market?" is Δ_{model, market_calibrated}(c) < 0 paired: the comparison that earns the phrase "the model is better in c". (Q-d) "Beyond unconditional history?" Δ_{model, horizon_empirical}(c) (and the pooled standardized empirical of the market record). (Q-e) "Is it the option information?" Δ_{arm B, price_only}(c) on the identical identities. (Q-f) "Is the market information additive?" the linear pool of model and market (market record G-L; later). A cell can satisfy Q-b and fail Q-c (the model does not exploit the gap) or satisfy Q-c and fail Q-b (the model beats a well-transported market through conditional information); the two are reported in separate columns and never merged into one word.

**3.4 Uncertainty.** Rows are dependent across names on the same expiry (the cross-section shares the terminal date) and within a name across overlapping origins (1.5). Interval of record: two-way cluster-robust standard error with clusters = expiry (settlement date) and symbol ([Cameron, Gelbach & Miller 2011](https://doi.org/10.1198/jbes.2010.07136); [Thompson 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2010.08.016); [Petersen 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhn053)), AND the existing date-block bootstrap (blocks 30 and 60 calendar days, 1,000 replicates, seed 829) which is the stationary-block analogue for overlapping monthly labels ([Politis & Romano 1994](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870)); report the wider. For a Diebold-Mariano-type paired test on the time series of daily mean differences use Newey-West with lag = the lead time's maximum sessions ([Diebold & Mariano 1995](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.1995.10524599); [Giacomini & White 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00718.x) for the conditional, rolling-window form that fits the expanding refits). PIT uniformity per sub-period ([Diebold, Gunther & Tay 1998](https://doi.org/10.2307/2527342); [Berkowitz 2001](https://doi.org/10.1198/07350010152596718)) is a calibration check, never a ranking.

**3.5 Multiplicity and the form of a claim.** Minimum support for any claim: n_c >= 30 realized events, >= 20 distinct expiries, >= 20 symbols, in EACH validation year; otherwise the cell is "insufficient support" (expected for |z| >= 3 under W28 and for most context-tercile tails). Within the primary family (14 cells) the paired p-values are Holm-adjusted ([Holm 1979](https://www.jstor.org/stable/4615733)); across all 196 cells Benjamini-Hochberg q-values are shown as a descriptive column ([Benjamini & Hochberg 1995](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.2517-6161.1995.tb02031.x)); the family-wise alternative over all recipes × cells is the stepdown of [Romano & Wolf 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2005.00615.x) / the SPA of [Hansen 2005](https://doi.org/10.1198/073500105000000063) on the bootstrap draws already produced, and the attempt count consumed by `max_bar` is the registry's, not the session's (ADR-0069). The selection bias of reporting the best-scoring cell is the same bias as reporting the best factor ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu 2016](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv059); [White 2000](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00152)): no table sorts cells by score, and every cell of the family appears in every table. Template of a claim: "Cell (M1, [−2, −1.5)): transported-market mass exceeds realized frequency by g = +x.x pp [CI] (two-way clustered), same sign in 4/4 years; the arm-B model's region Brier is lower than market_calibrated's by Δ = −y.y·10⁻⁴ [CI], 3/4 years; support n_c, expiries, symbols; primary family of 14, Holm-adjusted p = …; causes (i)-(iii) not separated." Never: "the market misprices" as a bare verdict, nor any quantity that is not a probability, a frequency or a proper score.

# Joint dependence model selection

## Question

Which dependence model should MIO test next?

## Finding

**Test a Gaussian copula with a few broad factors and a separate expiry-dependence block for each stock.** Compare it with the existing dense shrunken Gaussian, a Student t with the same dense dispersion, and independent uniforms. This experiment answers two different questions. Can pooling across stocks and expiries improve forecasts of joint moves? Does one market-wide shock that thickens both tails improve those forecasts? The available project evidence establishes that the existing sampler works on synthetic inputs. It provides no real-data ranking. The first deliverable should be an inventory of usable historical forecasts and an agreed joint scoring rule. Those determine which distinctions the data can support.

## The evidence comes from issuance dates

MIO needs a joint draw of future settlement prices across stocks and expiries offered on one decision date. All strikes and sides on one stock and expiry share its scenario price. Across stocks, an error in dependence changes the predicted breadth of a selloff. Within a stock, it changes the predicted difference between near and far expiry outcomes. A model can reproduce market-wide declines while materially understating that second risk. This target is a **physical distribution of terminal outcomes**. A vector of prices at several expiries by itself supplies neither a law for updating forecasts along an intermediate path nor a risk-neutral measure for pricing. [Frozen forecast-to-scenario design, stages 8–11](../../explanations/t-copula/source/claude-mio-stages-5-11.pdf)

The draft allows up to 1,328 stock–expiry coordinates and 881,128 pairs against roughly 1,070 **overlapping issuance dates**. These are design estimates. The locked roster of 166 stocks and its raw input-availability checks establish neither an archive of calibrated probability integral transforms (PITs) nor how many market episodes it covers. Under A0006, weekly names have original ranks 1–8 and monthly-only names have positions 1–3 with limited evidence. A Thursday holiday expiry retains its identity. Record the actual expiry, calendar days and trading sessions to settlement. Pooling a rank across dates assumes its dependence is sufficiently stable over those changing horizons. [Canonical decision Path, A0006 and A0009](../../decisioning/path.csv)

For each historical origin $a$, stock $n$ and original rank $\rho$, use the settled raw PIT $u_{n\rho\mid a}$ from the forecast issued at $a$. Its corrected value is $w_{n\rho\mid a}=\widehat\Gamma_{n,\rho,a}(u_{n\rho\mid a})$. At cutoff $d$, admit only $a<d$ and an earliest usable cutoff $d^\star_{n\rho\mid a}\le d$. Build vectors on the issuance date, retaining each historical map version. Distinguish unissued coordinates, pending settlements, missing maps and eligible observations. Estimate on observed subvectors or explicitly pooled common-origin moments. If stress changes which coordinates are observed, even a mathematically valid fit can describe a selected population. A cross-group association with no supporting overlap must be disclosed as an assumption. [Frozen design, stages 5–9](../../explanations/t-copula/source/claude-mio-stages-5-11.pdf) · [Xiong and Pelger on incomplete factor panels](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.08273)

## Separate broad market moves from expiry dependence

For the proposed Gaussian construction, let $i=(n,\rho)$, draw $K$ independent standard normal factors $F$, and draw an independent Gaussian residual vector $e$ with block-diagonal covariance $S=\operatorname{blockdiag}(S_1,\ldots,S_N)$. Each $S_n$ allows the errors of the same stock's expiries to move together after accounting for the broad factors. With loadings $B$, define

```math
 X=BF+e,\qquad H=BB^\top+S,\qquad
 R_{ij}=\frac{H_{ij}}{\sqrt{H_{ii}H_{jj}}},\qquad
 \tilde w_{n\rho}=\Phi\!\left(\frac{X_i}{\sqrt{H_{ii}}}\right).
```

Positive-definite residual blocks give a nonsingular joint law. The division by each coordinate's own standard deviation makes it standard normal, so its normal CDF is exactly uniform. Pool noisy block estimates toward a positive-definite template, with separate weekly and monthly templates where support permits. Begin with a market-wide factor and regularized exposures. Add a sector or maturity factor only if development data support a repeatable cross-stock pattern. Low-rank-plus-sparse covariance theory motivates this division of labor. The choice to put local dependence chiefly inside stock blocks is a project hypothesis. Dense Gaussian shrinkage earns its place because it can retain cross-stock residual associations that this restriction suppresses. [Fan, Liao and Mincheva on approximate factors](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.0175) · [Ledoit and Wolf on covariance shrinkage](https://ledoit.net/ole1a.pdf)

Every candidate supplies the same input to the frozen price transformation. For scenario $\omega$ and $t=t_{n\rho\mid d}$, use

```math
 \tilde p_{nt}(\omega)=\widehat\Psi_{nt}^{-1}\!\left(
 \widehat\Gamma_{n,\rho,d}^{-1}\!\left(\tilde w_{n\rho}(\omega)\right)
 \mid\mathcal F_d\right).
```

First invert the **current** cell correction, then invert the **current** price forecast. The physical marginal can therefore be heavy-tailed or skewed even under Gaussian dependence. Dropping inactive cells from a fixed joint draw gives the active subset its corresponding marginal law. Refitting each subset on a different set of complete historical dates can change that law. [Frozen design, stages 10–11](../../explanations/t-copula/source/claude-mio-stages-5-11.pdf)

## Let the pattern of errors choose the challenger

Compare **dense versus structured Gaussian** on identical inputs and, as far as feasible, the same estimation criterion and tuning budget. This tests the dispersion architecture. Compare **dense Gaussian versus dense t at identical fitted $R$** to isolate the tail family. A finite-degree-of-freedom t scales the whole Gaussian vector by one random multiplier, then applies its univariate t CDF coordinatewise. Its lower and upper limiting tail dependence agree for each pair. Their magnitude can vary across pairs with $R_{ij}$. Gaussian tail dependence tends to zero for nonperfect correlations, yet Gaussian scenarios can still produce frequent joint losses at practical thresholds. If the t advantage appears sensitive to the architecture, add a matched structured t to complete the comparison. [Demarta and McNeil on the t copula](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1751-5823.2005.tb00254.x)

**Keep an empirical benchmark on a small, fixed panel with enough complete common-origin history.** For $m$ training origins, rank each coordinate across those origins, handling ties reproducibly. Draw one historical origin for the whole vector and jitter each coordinate uniformly inside its historical rank interval $((r-1)/m,r/m)$. Each column contains every rank once, giving continuous uniform margins and retaining the historical combinations of rank intervals. This checkerboard cannot populate unseen combinations of those intervals. Score the other models on exactly the same panel as well. A panel chosen for its coverage can test dependence within that cohort, with limited reach beyond it. [Segers, Sibuya and Tsukahara on empirical copulas](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.04430) · [Copulas.jl empirical constructions](https://lrnv.github.io/Copulas.jl/stable/bestiary/empirical)

Choose the next addition from the pattern of **development** errors. Repeated underprediction of both market-wide gains and losses favors testing symmetric common-scale tails. A mismatch confined to particular groups on both sides could motivate a grouped t. Classical grouped t models can vary group tail thickness while sharing an underlying scale driver and retaining directional symmetry. Repeated excess *downside* breadth after auditing conditional marginal calibration would instead justify one sign-anchored asymmetric market shock with pooled residual tails. Oh and Patton found stronger crash dependence in S&P 100 daily stock returns. That result makes asymmetry plausible, but its size and stability in corrected multi-expiry forecast errors remain unknown. [Hintz, Hofert and Lemieux on grouped scales](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9091/8/4/103) · [Oh and Patton’s equity factor-copula study](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Oh_Patton_MV_factor_copula_JBES_2017.pdf)

For an additive non-Gaussian factor model, transform **each full latent sum** $X_i$ using its own implied CDF $G_i$. With one independent factor and residual, $G_i(x)=\mathbb E_F[G_{e_i}(x-b_iF)]$. Then $G_i(X_i)$ is uniform for a continuous sum. Applying a plain Gaussian or t CDF to an arbitrary sum can distort every downstream price marginal. Numerical integration or an independent reference simulation requires an accuracy check in both tails. Time variation is a separate choice for any architecture or tail family. Test one issue-time observable conditioning variable only if it predicts repeatable residual changes against the same static model. Retrospective crash labels are exploratory residual diagnostics only; they must never serve as issue-time features or determine evaluation subsets or promotion criteria. [Oh and Patton on latent factor constructions](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Oh_Patton_MV_factor_copula_JBES_2017.pdf) · [Dynamic factor copulas](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Oh_Patton_JBES_2018.pdf)

If the errors are localized to conditional pairs, reserve a shallow vine with a fixed graph for a covered panel. It can distinguish pair-specific upper and lower dependence that a common shock misses. A full 1,328-dimensional vine has 881,128 pair slots, and automatic tree selection can examine many pairs even with truncation. Conditional edges need simultaneous histories of their conditioning variables. A flat Archimedean family imposes exchangeability across stock and expiry labels. A hierarchy relaxes this at the price of nesting restrictions. Flexible neural generators add marginal-CDF estimation and tail extrapolation demands. None has a demonstrated whole-universe advantage on these data. [Dißmann et al. on vine structure selection](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.2002) · [McNeil and Nešlehová on Archimedean copulas](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3750) · [Janke et al. on generative copulas](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/dac4a67bdc4a800113b0f1ad67ed696f-Paper.pdf)

## Freeze the comparison before looking at outcomes

**First, inventory metadata under the existing Path.** Identify pinned forecast and correction-map manifests, usable dates, original ranks, maturity timestamps, mask reasons and common-origin coverage by cell and pair. Preserve A0009’s roster and disclose its unmeasured survivorship bias. A0003 sets 2017–2019 development and 2020–2023 validation with boundary settlement rules. A0008 pins the quarterly forecasts from 2019Q4 through 2023Q4. The first quarter in that series is part of the tuned year. If adequate pre-validation corrected forecasts cannot be documented, report the resulting tuning limit. Development windows alone do not supply development PITs. The forecasts and scores have access restrictions, 2024–2025 is sealed, and 2026 stays sealed. A0010 limits MIO to its owner-controlled sequence through 2023. Historical model selection also overlaps that period. This proposed comparison requires agreement on access and scoring before any outcome work. [Canonical decision Path, A0003 and A0008–A0010](../../decisioning/path.csv)

**Second, agree on a small joint forecast scorecard.** A0002 specifies marginal weighted and full CRPS, strike Brier scores and calibration diagnostics. A0005 and A0007 address marginal promotion and eligibility. For a dependence comparison, keep the forecast margins fixed across candidates and check their conditional calibration by rank, actual horizon and issue-time state through the authorized workflow. Freeze masks, scale, weights, tuning budget, thresholds and success criteria in advance. Use a sample-based joint energy score on the shared observed vector, with a variogram score emphasizing same-stock expiry and selected cross-stock pairs. Add CRPS for a fixed signed near-versus-far return projection, and Brier scores for advance-defined downside and upside breadth events, for example a given fraction of a fixed panel crossing its 10% or 90% marginal quantile. These are forecasting targets. The joint scores and their role in promotion require separate agreement. [Canonical decision Path, A0002, A0005 and A0007](../../decisioning/path.csv) · [Fissler and Hoga on fixed-margin forecast comparison](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04165) · [Scheuerer and Hamill on dependence-sensitive scoring](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/22327/noaa_22327_DS1.pdf)

**Third, compare forecasts issued before their outcomes.** Use identical origins, coordinates, scenario budgets and projection definitions for each comparison. Report common-panel coverage alongside results. Evaluate joint log density secondarily among candidates with evaluable densities on that same observed subvector. Pairwise scores alone leave higher-order breadth unidentified. Estimate uncertainty from paired origin-level score differences using contiguous time blocks that preserve cross-stock vectors and span material target overlap. Check block-length sensitivity, shared expiries, yearly stability, and results after leaving out each major stress episode. Even 1,070 fully observed calibrated origins imply only 10.7 expected *marginal* events at 1%. The number of independent stress episodes can be far smaller. Report event counts and intervals. Prefer the simpler adequate construction if precision supports equivalence within an agreed tolerance. If rankings reverse or intervals cover material gains and losses, leave the default undecided. [Politis and Romano on time-block resampling](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870) · [Oh and Patton’s market-breadth analysis](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Oh_Patton_MV_factor_copula_JBES_2017.pdf)

## Julia feasibility leaves room for the statistical choice

The existing native synthetic benchmark at design scale recorded a warm fit of about **0.54 seconds**, about **0.48 seconds for 2,048 draws**, and **0.74 GiB peak RSS for the process**. Its degrees-of-freedom criterion used 64 scoring pairs. Speed alone supplies little reason to replace this candidate. Copulas.jl v1.0.1 supports several complete-data families and empirical copulas, with a Julia 1.11 floor. VineCopulas.jl v0.1.2 requires Copulas.jl 0.1.40 through the 0.1 series. Its released truncated C/D-vine sampler is available. The general R-vine sampler requires full depth. Development main accepts Copulas.jl 1.x. This research installed no packages and fitted no models. The next engineering decision depends on which forecast comparison the coverage audit makes possible.

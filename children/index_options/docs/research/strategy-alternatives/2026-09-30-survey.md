# Index-option strategies beyond the iron condor: evidence survey

Tags: **[PR]** peer-reviewed · **[WP]** working paper · **[EX]** Cboe-published
or Cboe-commissioned · **[V]** vendor/practitioner · **[calc]** computed here
from Cboe's public daily index files. Numbers are as reported by the source
unless tagged [calc]. Nothing here is a backtest of this child, and nothing
authorizes trading.

## Question

The child trades one structure: a defined-risk iron condor, 30-60 DTE, held
to settlement. On real data, VIX-implied strikes earned about +$79/trade
(t = 1.32, hit rate 92%, CVaR5 -$3,795). Strikes chosen by the ML forecasts
did not beat them, HAR adds nothing detectable over VIX, and the
predictive-CDF work gains only about 0.8% CRPS over the empirical baseline.

Which other index-option strategies have credible evidence of positive
expected return? Which of them can this child model with the data it holds:
SPY/QQQ/IWM end-of-day chains for 2008-2025; the SPX/XSP chain recorder
from Sep 2026; daily Cboe history for SPX, RUT, VIX1D, VIX9D, VIX, VIX3M,
VIX6M, VIX1Y, VVIX, SKEW, VXN, RVX and others; and the predictive CDF for
each exact expiry?

Five parallel literature searches covered:

1. premium-selling variants;
2. timing and conditioning signals;
3. long convexity and tail hedges;
4. P-vs-Q payoff selection;
5. short-dated options and implementation costs.

The consequential claims were spot-checked (see Verification).

## Finding

### 0. The premise: the unconditional premium has mostly gone since ~2010

- **Dew-Becker & Giglio (2025) [WP, Chicago Fed WP 2025-17]:** "over the
  past 15 years, option alphas have become indistinguishable from zero".
  - Traded 5% OTM S&P puts had cumulative returns of about zero from Mar 2009 to Dec 2022.
  - Their proposed cause is intermediaries: dealer net gamma moved from negative to zero or positive.
- **Heston & Todorov (AFA 2024) [WP]:** the realized S&P variance risk premium (VRP), measured with tradable replicating portfolios, was not significantly different from zero over 2006-2020.
- **Cboe CNDR [calc].** CNDR is Cboe's iron-condor benchmark: sell 20Δ, buy 5Δ, monthly, hold, collateral in T-bills. Returns below include the T-bill interest on collateral.

  | Period | CAGR | Vol | t (mean > 0) | Notes |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 1986-2006 (back-test) | 9.8% | 7.0% | 6.3 | |
  | 2007 - Sep 2026 | 0.9% | 6.8% | 0.70 | Max drawdown -19.7% |
  | Since 2010 | 0.4% | 6.6% | 0.41 | |
  | Since launch (Aug 2015) | 1.0% | 6.2% | 0.73 | |

  - Since 2007 that is at or below T-bills.
  - The independent agent computation gave an excess return of -0.4%/yr (t -0.28) over 2007-2026. Its CAPM alpha for 1986-2006 was +4.2%/yr (t 2.9).
  - CNDR holds T-bills worth 10× the maximum option loss, so a -10% month is about one full max loss.
- **Cboe PUT, the ATM cash-secured put-write:**
  - [calc] Since 2010 it earned 8.2% CAGR at 9.5% vol, versus 12.1% for SPX (price only). Its Sharpe ratio is about equal to the equity's.
  - Agent-computed CAPM alpha: +0.98%/yr (t 0.79) for 2007-2026 and +0.09%/yr (t 0.06) since 2015.
  - **What remains is equity beta.**
- **Implication for this child:** the +$79/trade at t = 1.32 is what the post-2010 literature predicts for an unconditional condor. Another fixed *shape* will not fix it. Any lift must come from:
  - **which side** is sold;
  - **when** to sell;
  - **how much** to sell;
  - **relative value** across strikes, tenors and indices.

### 1. Premium-selling variants

**Published benchmarks, 1986-2018 (Wilshire 2019 [EX])**

| BXMD | 10.22% | 12.8% | 0.55 | -42.7% |
| CMBO | 9.20% | 10.9% | 0.55 | -38.1% |
| PPUT | 6.64% | 12.1% | 0.29 | -38.9% |
| S&P 500 | 9.80% | 14.9% | 0.45 | -50.9% |

- **Bondarenko 2019 [EX], on PUT:**
  - Skew -2.09 and kurtosis 12.6.
  - Down-market beta 0.75 against up-market beta 0.34, i.e. a concave payoff.
  - Over 2006-2018 the monthly alpha of PUT was 0.05% and of WPUT -0.01%.
- **2007 - Aug 2026, agent-computed from Cboe files:**

  | Strategy | CAGR | Sharpe | Max drawdown |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | PUT | 7.1% | 0.55 | -37.1% |
  | WPUT | 4.3% | 0.32 | -28.6% |
  | BXMD | 8.4% | 0.57 | -47.0% |
  | BFLY (short ATM straddle, 5% wings) | -2.4% | -0.30 | -54.9% |
  | PUTR (Russell 2000 put-write) | 6.6% | 0.41 | -46.2% |
  | SPX (price only) | 8.9% | 0.53 | -56.8% |

**The put side carries the premium; the call side does not**

- **Bondarenko 2014 [PR]** (S&P futures options, 1987-2000): ATM puts earned -39%/month and 6% OTM puts -95%/month. Call returns were "generally positive but not statistically significant".
- **Constantinides, Jackwerth & Savov 2013 [PR, RAPS]:**
  - Leverage-adjusted long-call CAPM alphas are negative, e.g. -0.74%/month for 30-day 105% calls.
  - A short 95% put earned +0.66%/month.
  - Crash and volatility-jump factors explain both.
- **Israelov & Tummala 2017 [WP, AQR]:** front-month puts, from near-the-money to moderately OTM, are the best options to sell; puts beat calls.
- **Israelov & Nielsen 2015, "Covered Calls Uncovered" [PR, FAJ]:**
  - The short-vol component had a Sharpe near 1.0 but carried under 10% of the risk.
  - The drifting equity delta carried much more risk and was uncompensated.
  - A condor held to expiry carries the same drifting delta.
- **Inference, untested here:** the call spread collects little premium and has negative beta in a rising market.

**Wings buy the most overpriced options**

- Deep OTM puts are the worst-returning options on the surface (-50% to -95%/month raw; CJS 2013 and Bondarenko 2014).
- **Santa-Clara & Saretto 2009 [PR, JFM], 1985-2002:**
  - Short 10% OTM strangles earned +52.7%/month gross, a monthly Sharpe of 0.548.
  - Bid/ask costs cut Sharpe "substantially, sometimes negative".
  - Modeled margin calls turned a near-maturity strangle from +2.7%/month to -1.0%/month.
- Defined risk (wings) avoids forced exits but pays away premium.

**Delta and tenor**

- **Spintwig [V]** SPX 45-DTE put verticals held to expiry, 2007-2024. Margin was sized in hindsight, so treat these as upper bounds.

  | Put vertical (short Δ / long Δ) | CAGR | Sharpe | Max drawdown | Win rate |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 10/5 | 8.3% | 0.82 | -31.9% | 96.5% |
  | 16/5 | 9.2% | 0.86 | -34.8% | 92.8% |
  | 30/16 | 8.3% | 0.53 | -52.5% | — |

  - Their 16/5 iron condor had a Sharpe of 0.62.
- **Johnson 2017 [PR, JFQA]**, long ATM S&P straddles by maturity, 1996-2013:

  | Maturity | Monthly return | Sharpe |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 month | -5.29% | -0.51 |
  | 3 months | -1.70% | -0.36 |
  | 6 months | -0.42% | -0.13 |
  | 12 months | +0.13% | +0.05 |

- **Dew-Becker, Giglio, Le & Rodriguez 2017 [PR, JFE]:** only short-horizon, transitory variance is priced. Variance claims beyond about two months earn roughly zero.
- **The child's 30-60 DTE window is in the priced zone.**

**Management rules**

- There is no peer-reviewed evidence for the "take profit at 50%" or "exit at 21 DTE" rules.
- **Blom [V]** (16Δ strangles, 2001-2020): Sharpe 0.52 when managed versus 0.67 passive.
- Holding to expiry was about the best rule in vendor tests.

### 2. Timing and conditioning (when and how much to sell)

**VIX term-structure slope: the strongest peer-reviewed evidence**

- **Johnson 2017 [PR, JFQA]:**
  - SLOPE, the second principal component of the VIX term structure (1-12 months), carries essentially all the VRP information. The VIX *level* is insignificant once SLOPE is included.
  - The effect is non-linear. In the bottom SLOPE quintile (curve inverted), 17 of 18 long-variance assets had positive mean returns.
  - Out-of-sample R² for next-month straddles was 2.3% (1-month maturity) to 10.4% (9-month).
  - Real-time rule, with PCA refit on data up to each day: long straddles in the bottom quintile, short otherwise. It beat always-short by 11.9%/yr over 2000-2013. That uses midpoint prices, and much of the gain came in late 2008, though the effect survives dropping 2008-09.
- **Simon & Campasano 2014 [PR, J. Derivatives]**, VIX futures 2006-2011:
  - Short in contango: +$539/trade after costs.
  - Long in backwardation: +$908/trade.
- **Takeaway:** "stand aside when inverted" is robust; "go long when inverted" is weaker.

**The sign of the VRP**

- **Cheng 2019 [PR, RFS]**, VIX futures 2004-2015. The VRP is measured as the futures price minus an ARMA forecast of VIX.
  - The real-time rule "short only when the premium is positive", traded at bid/ask, earned Sharpe 0.87 versus 0.57 for always-short.
  - The premium falls or stays flat when risk rises.
- **Carr & Wu 2009 [PR, RFS]**, 1996-2003:
  - SPX realized vol 18.8 against a variance-swap rate of 24.4.
  - For NDX (37.5 vs 40.2) the gap was not significant.
- **Israelov & Nielsen 2015, "Still Not Cheap" [PR, JPM]**, 1990-2014:
  - Implied minus realized vol averaged +3.4 points and was positive 88% of the time.
  - It was still +2.5 points in the lowest VIX decile.
- **Consequence for this child:** HAR adds nothing over VIX, so the forecast E[RV] ≈ a + b·VIX² and a model-based VRP is roughly the VIX level, which Johnson finds useless. The usable VRP information is its **sign after shocks** (trailing realized vol above VIX) plus the term structure.
- **Level is unresolved:**
  - Bondarenko [EX] and Malkiel et al. 2018 [PR] find higher VIX gives higher put-write returns, but the worst months also sit in the top VIX quintile.
  - Johnson finds level insignificant after SLOPE.

**VVIX and SKEW**

- **Park 2015 [PR, JFM]**, 2006-2012: high VVIX predicts lower returns on OTM SPX puts and VIX calls over the next 3-4 weeks (R² 2-5%). This is in-sample and dominated by 2008.
- **SKEW** is noisy in most studies; only a decomposed "negative SKEW" component predicts downturns (Bevilacqua & Tunaru 2021 [PR]). Treat SKEW as a placebo.

**Sizing**

- **Moreira & Muir 2017 [PR]:** managing exposure by volatility works for equity factors; options were not tested.
- **Cederburg et al. 2020 [PR, JFE]:** across 103 strategies, the real-time versions generally do *not* beat unmanaged ones.
- **Yang 2024 [WP]:** cutting short-vol exposure after high vol raises a variance-swap Sharpe from 1.54 to 1.76 (per the abstract only).
- **Newfound [V]:** option-strategy results depend on roll dates ("timing luck"). Staggered weekly tranches reduce this.

**Events and non-trading periods**

- **Londono & Samadi 2023 [WP, Fed IFDP 1376]:** SPX expiries that span a release carry ATM IV 44 bp (GDP) to 134 bp (FOMC) higher. The FOMC-spanning VRP is 63 bp higher.
- **Wright 2020 [WP, NBER]:** the event-day VRP is positive, largest for FOMC.
- **Lucca & Moench 2015 [PR, JF]:** the pre-FOMC equity drift, which largely disappeared after 2015.
- **Jones & Shemesh 2018 [PR, JF]:** SPX option returns are more negative over non-trading periods (puts -1.07%, t -4.56).
- **Muravyev & Ni 2020 [PR, JFE]:** delta-hedged SPX options earn -1% close-to-open and +0.3% open-to-close. The premium is earned **overnight**.
- For 30-60 DTE trades every window spans events, so the only lever is entry date relative to an event (untested).

**Cross-index**

- There is no evidence that the premium is larger on RUT, NDX, IWM or QQQ.
- RVX minus RUT realized vol averaged 3.3 points over 2004-2016 [EX], against about 4.2 points for SPX over a different period.
- **Driessen, Maenhout & Vilkov 2009 [PR, JF]:** index VRP is a correlation premium (S&P 100 implied correlation 46.7% vs realized 28.7%). Dispersion trading was not exploitable with frictions, and this child has no single-stock chains.

### 3. Long convexity and tail hedges (the "positive upside" question)

**No standalone positive EV for bought protection**

- **Israelov 2019, "Pathetic Protection" [PR, JAI]**, 1986-2016:
  - PPUT earned 2.5%/yr excess return versus 5.8% for SPX.
  - A 36.5% SPX / 63.5% cash mix matched that return at half the volatility, with a *smaller* worst-1% 20-day drawdown (-6.6% vs -9.6%).
  - Put alpha -1.8%/yr (t -2.0).
- **AQR 2020 [V/WP]:** rolling 5% OTM 1-month puts, levered to 10% vol, earned -6.4%/yr (Sharpe -0.61, max drawdown -92%). Multi-asset trend earned +8.7%/yr (Sharpe 0.84).
- **Harvey, Van Hemert et al. 2019 [PR, JPM]:** continuously held short-dated SPX puts earned -7.4%/yr (1985-2018). They paid in all seven crises, but crises are 14% of the time.
- **Szado 2020 [EX]:** 1%/month allocations to a 60/40 portfolio, 2006-2017. Near-the-money SPX puts hurt least (ATM +4.5%/yr); 25%-OTM puts (-2.8%) and VIX calls (about 4%) hurt most.
- **Universa's "+3,612%"** return on "required invested capital" is unaudited and not reproducible.

**Long straddles**

- **Coval & Shumway 2001 [PR, JF]:** zero-beta ATM straddles lose about 3%/week.
- **Israelov & Tummala 2020 [PR, JAI]:** 1-month SPX options profit only in the *top* decile of realized-vol changes, and lose in the bottom seven.
- Buying before events loses to the event VRP (Wright).
- **The one index exception is Johnson's bottom SLOPE quintile:** next-day straddle returns of +0.44%/day (1-month maturity). That horizon is one day, so a 30-60 DTE hold into vol mean-reversion must be tested.

**Ratio spreads**

- A put backspread is long the worst-returning options, so it is negative EV and a hedge only.
- A front ratio has positive EV but a naked tail, which violates defined risk.
- A broken-wing fly is a condor variant.
- **Kozhan, Neuberger & Schneider 2013 [PR, RFS]:** the skew premium is about 42% of implied skew, but skew trades hedged for variance earn an insignificant premium. Skew is mostly the same factor as variance.
- **Vilkov's corrected 0DTE put-ratio net Sharpe is -0.61** (§5).

**Calls**

- **Bakshi, Madan & Panayotov 2010 [PR, JFE]:** OTM call returns fall with strike and turn negative from about 10% OTM.
- **Frazzini & Pedersen 2022 [PR, RAPS]:** long low-embedded-leverage, short high-embedded-leverage index options earned 7.5-11.5%/month (t 6.3).
- **Hypothesis (untested anywhere found):** a long ATM/ITM call with a short 5-10% OTM call carries the equity premium with a floored loss. That is beta, not a convexity edge.

**The decisive test for any hedge sleeve (Israelov)**

- Compare condor + hedge against a *smaller condor at the same CVaR5*.
- Hold-to-expiry understates long convexity, because it gives gains back through vol crush. Test a monetization variant on end-of-day marks.

### 4. P-vs-Q payoff selection: turning the predictive CDF into trades

**Identity (derived by integration by parts)**

For a European payoff g built from listed strikes,

    E_P[g] - E_Q[g] = ∫ g'(s) · (F_Q(s) - F_P(s)) ds.

- A vertical spread ≈ ΔK · [P(S>K) - Q(S>K)]. It is a bet on the **CDF**, the quantity the model forecasts best.
- A butterfly ≈ ΔK² · [f_P(K) - f_Q(K)]. It is a bet on the **density**, the derivative of the CDF, so noise is amplified.
- CRPS rewards whole-CDF accuracy, not pointwise density. **Prefer wide verticals and condor wings to narrow flies.**
- The gap F_Q − F_P = risk premium + model error. The empirical baseline already holds the unconditional premium; ML can add only *conditional* variation. That fits the observed ~0.8% CRPS gain and the model strikes not beating VIX strikes.

**Closest published template**

- **Faias & Santa-Clara 2017 [PR, JFQA]:**
  - Four 1-month SPX options (ATM and 5% OTM call and put), held to expiry.
  - The P model bootstraps standardized returns since 1950, rescaled by current realized vol.
  - The objective is CRRA with γ = 10, set high to limit overfitting. Longs are bought at the ask and shorts sold at the bid.
  - Out of sample, Jan 1996 - Aug 2013: Sharpe 0.82 and certainty equivalent 9.94%/yr, against the market's 0.29 and -1.31%.
  - Returns are positively skewed with beta ≈ 0.03, and the strategy is sometimes net long options.
  - The authors attribute returns to relative mispricing, not to premia.
  - **Caveat:** the sample ends before most of the post-2010 decline in §0.

**Supporting results**

- **Driessen & Maenhout 2007 [PR, RoF]:** CRRA and loss-averse investors optimally short OTM puts and ATM straddles, even after costs, margins and peso-problem corrections.
- **Aït-Sahalia & Brandt 2008 [WP, NBER]:** the optimal terminal payoff is W*(s) ∝ (p(s)/q(s))^{1/γ}. Carr-Madan spanning maps it to listed strikes.
- **Bakshi, Madan & Panayotov 2010:** q/p is high in *both* tails (a humped P/Q ratio), which makes the CRRA-optimal payoff condor-like.
  - The condor *shape* is therefore roughly right.
  - **The gains are in conditional width, asymmetry and size.**
- **Kostakis, Panigirtzoglou & Skiadopoulos 2011 [PR, Mgmt Sci]:** a risk-adjusted risk-neutral density used as P beat historical distributions out of sample after costs. It is a strong competing P model and blend input.
- **Aït-Sahalia, Wang & Yared 2001 [PR]:** skewness and kurtosis trades had Sharpe 0.25-0.31 over 1986-96, with a peso-problem caveat.

**Extracting the risk-neutral density**

- **Figlewski 2010 [PR chapter]:**
  - Use OTM options only.
  - Smooth in implied-vol space with a spline of 4th order or higher; cubic splines create density spikes.
  - Keep the fit within bid/ask and attach GEV tails.
- **Gatheral & Jacquier 2014 [PR]:** SSVI gives arbitrage-free surfaces for daily fits across many expiries.
- **Bliss & Panigirtzoglou 2004 [PR, JF]:** the utility-tilted implied P has a relative risk aversion of about 3.5, and higher at 1-2 weeks.
- **For SPY/QQQ/IWM**, convert American options to European-equivalent IVs first.

**Calendars**

- A calendar's value at the front expiry depends on the back-month IV, not only on S_T1, so it needs a joint (S_T1, IV_T1) model. This is phase 2.
- **Santa-Clara & Saretto:** calendars were "less impressive" than straight short-vol trades.

### 5. Short-dated options (0DTE, 1DTE, weeklies)

**0DTE**

- **Vilkov, "0DTE Trading Rules" [WP + MIT code]**, SPXW 2016-2026:
  - The repo's KNOWN-ISSUES file (Aug 2026) records a unit bug that charged the half-spread at 1/100 of its size.
  - Corrected net Sharpe ratios:

    | Structure | Corrected net Sharpe |
    |---|---|
    | Iron fly/condor | -2.67 |
    | Put ratio | -0.61 |
    | Bear put | -0.73 |
    | Long strangle | -0.97 |
    | Risk reversal | +0.10 |

  - The repo states: "No structure retains a materially positive net Sharpe ratio."
- **Almeida, Freire & Hizmeri 2025 [WP]:**
  - Annualized VRP: 1.5-3.0% at 0DTE, versus 0.55% at 1DTE and 0.81% at 22DTE.
  - The 0DTE premium is upside-driven.
  - Always writing delta-hedged ATM 0DTE options is mostly negative net of bid/ask, and the mispricing faded after 2022.
- **Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda [WP]:**
  - Retail 0DTE losses were over $125M, more than $90M of it transaction costs.
  - Retail iron condors: median return +5.5%, mean -1.1%, 5th percentile -100%.
- **Bandi, Fusari & Renò [PR, forthcoming JF]:** a 0DTE pricing model, not a returns study.

**Weeklies versus monthlies**

- **Bondarenko [EX]**, gross, 2006-2018: WPUT Sharpe 0.40 versus PUT 0.50, even though WPUT collected 37.1% versus 22.1% premium a year.
- Per-leg dollar costs do not shrink with tenor, so cost as a share of premium rises roughly as 1/√T (inference).

**1DTE**

- **Muravyev & Ni:** the premium is earned overnight, so close-to-settlement 1DTE holds are the short-dated variant that captures it.
- **Johannes et al. [WP]:** 1DTE returns are insignificant on most days and significant on macro days (magnitudes unverified).
- **Data limit:** daily SPY/QQQ expiries exist only from Nov 2022 (IWM from Apr 2024), and VIX1D history only from May 2022.

**Data granularity**

- End-of-day chains suffice for close-entry, hold-to-settlement strategies: 1DTE, weeklies, and 30-60 DTE.
- Intraday data is needed for 0DTE after the open, stops, profit targets and hedging.

### 6. Implementation facts a fair comparison must encode

**Costs**

- Fill at bid/ask as the base case, with sensitivity runs at mid - 25/50/75% of the half-spread.
- **Muravyev & Pearson 2020 [PR, RFS]:** traders who time executions pay under 40% of the conventional spread. Treat that as an optimistic bound.
- **Santa-Clara & Saretto:** round-trip spreads were about 10%, and near-expiry 5% OTM puts 13.9%. Holding to expiry pays only half.

**Fees** (tastytrade schedule, 2026 [V]; keep them as config, not code)

| Item | Fee |
|---|---|
| Commission | $1 to open, $0 to close |
| Clearing | $0.10 |
| SPX exchange fee | $0.60 |
| XSP exchange fee | $0 below 10 lots, $0.07 at 10+ |
| ORF | $0.02 |

**Margin and capital**

- **Reg-T:** a spread is margined at max risk minus credit. An iron condor is margined on the riskier side only.
- A cash-secured put ties up the full strike.
- Compare strategies on return on max loss, per capital-day, with T-bill interest on collateral.

**Tax**

- SPX and XSP are Section 1256 contracts: 60/40 long/short-term treatment and year-end mark-to-market.
- SPY, QQQ and IWM options are short-term.

**Settlement and assignment**

- SPX monthlies settle on the AM opening quotation (SOQ).
- SPXW and XSP settle PM at the official close.
- SPY, QQQ and IWM options are American and physically settled: model early assignment of short ITM calls before ex-dividend dates.

**Timing**

- Cboe vol indices update until about 16:15 ET, while the index closes at 16:00. Use the t-1 signal.
- Non-expiring SPX options also trade to 16:15, so closing quotes can be stale.
- Drop zero-bid and crossed quotes, and log missing strikes.

**Inference**

- Overlapping entries inflate t-statistics. Use non-overlapping tranches or HAC standard errors with lag at least the holding period, plus a block bootstrap.
- Deflate for the number of rules tried.
- Report results pre/post 2010 and pre/post May 2022, and with and without the top three events.

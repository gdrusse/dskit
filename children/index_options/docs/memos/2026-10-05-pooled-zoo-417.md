# Pooled model zoo over 408 stocks at DTE 31: results (2026-10-05)

## Headline (TL;DR)

No architecture reliably beat the per-ticker empirical CDF out of sample. Over
nine scored folds (Feb 2024 to Dec 2025; 393 tickers, 103,961 forecasts), the
best pick, the CNN on base features, lowered weighted CRPS by 0.36% (95%
interval +0.02 to +0.85). That is the only interval clear of zero, and it does
not survive a Holm or Bonferroni adjustment across the 10 picks. The other base
models scored between −0.03% and +0.12%, all with intervals spanning zero.
Option-trade features made every architecture worse, by 0.25 to 1.36 points,
and the GRU and LSTM harms survive the adjustment.

**Loss.** Every candidate minimizes the same weighted CRPS. Let $y$ be the
scaled return and $F$ the forecast CDF:

```math
S(F,y)=\mathrm{CRPS}(F,y)+1.0\cdot\mathrm{tailCRPS}(F,y),\qquad \text{tail}=[-2.5,-0.5]\cup[0.5,2.5]
```

Selection and reporting use this same `weighted_crps`. Skill compares each
model against `horizon_empirical`, which is each ticker's own empirical return
CDF (401 knots) refit on every fold's training rows. Here $\bar S_i$ is ticker
$i$'s mean score over its scored rows, and $N$ is the number of tickers:

```math
\text{skill}=100\left(1-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\bar S_i^{\text{model}}}{\bar S_i^{\text{emp}}}\right)
```

Pooled skill is therefore the equal-weight mean of per-ticker skill.

### Multiple testing across the 10 picks

The family is the 10 frozen picks against `horizon_empirical`, tested two-sided
at a 5% familywise error rate. Bonferroni allocates 0.005 to each pick. Holm
steps down on the same budget. Both hold under any dependence between the
picks, and the picks share block draws because the seed is common.

The replicates are not stored, so I reran the study's own resampling (seed 829,
1,000 replicates) from `study/report/scores.parquet`. All 20 `weighted_crps`
intervals matched `comparison.json` exactly. With $k$ the number of replicates
on the far side of zero, the two-sided p-value is $p = 2k/1000$.

| Pick (60-date blocks) | $k$ | p | Bonferroni | Holm | 99.5% interval |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| GRU options | 0 | ≤ 0.002 | ≤ 0.02 | ≤ 0.02 | [−1.77, −0.15] |
| LSTM options | 1 | 0.002 | 0.02 | 0.018 | [−2.59, −0.14] |
| MLP options | 5 | 0.010 | 0.10 | 0.080 | [−2.38, +0.07] |
| CNN base | 17 | 0.034 | 0.34 | 0.238 | [−0.13, +1.04] |
| the other six | — | ≥ 0.12 | 1.00 | ≥ 0.72 | all span zero |

For the CNN base: $p=2\times17/1000=0.034$. Bonferroni gives $10\times0.034=0.34$,
and Holm gives $7\times0.034=0.238$. Both are above 0.05, and the 99.5% interval
spans zero. **The CNN gain does not survive.** With 30-date blocks the result is
the same: $k=10$, so p = 0.020, Holm 0.14, and the 99.5% interval is
[−0.03, +0.99]. At 60-date blocks, the GRU and LSTM option harms survive Holm.
At 30-date blocks, the MLP option harm also survives (Holm 0.048). One thousand
replicates resolve p only to 0.002, and each 0.25% tail of a 99.5% interval
holds only about 2.5 draws.

## Interpretation

**Supported by the evidence:**

- At DTE 31 and this scale, none of the five architectures is distinguishable
  from the per-ticker empirical CDF once the 10 picks are considered together.
  At best, the gain is a few tenths of a percent of weighted CRPS.
- **Warm-up scores shrank.** The four torch base picks averaged +1.42% in the
  warm-up and +0.14% when scored. The rank correlation between warm-up and
  scored skill across the 10 picks is 0.03, and the order inverted: GRU was
  first in the warm-up and CNN was first when scored. The twin-noise of up to
  0.94 points exceeds every scored effect, so one warm-up fold cannot rank
  these models.
- **Option features hurt**, in every architecture and in both loss terms. The
  harm survives multiplicity for GRU and LSTM. This matches the earlier top-5
  pooled study (`~/wt/option-universe-300/.../pooled-heads-top5`, plain-CRPS
  selection, 5 tickers). There the options variants scored −3.29% (GRU,
  60-date [−6.48, −0.30]) and −5.50% (MLP), against +0.94% and −0.35% for base,
  and lost to base on all 5 tickers. The penalty here is smaller (−1.05 to −1.36
  points for torch, −0.25 for LightGBM) but has the same sign. In both studies,
  the options variants looked competitive in the warm-up: here +1.22 to +1.56
  for the torch picks, and in the top-5 study the GRU options scored +4.73
  against +2.77 for base.

**Not supported:**

- That the CNN is better than the other encoders. Its gain does not survive
  adjustment, and most of it comes from 21 short-history tickers.
- That pooling helps generally. The short-history gain is consistent with a
  per-ticker empirical reference fitted on few rows, which any pooled model can
  beat. The run does not test that.
- **That option-trade data carry no signal.** The run does not isolate why the
  features hurt. One candidate is visible in the panel. The observed share of
  option features is 0% in the warm-up, 0% to 17.9% of training rows in scored
  folds 33–41, and 83.8% to 93.5% of scored rows. Under that plan, the models
  are mostly trained on imputed constants and then scored on real values.

## Caveats

- **Single seed (11).** The warm-up twins differ by up to 0.94 points from
  training noise alone. Fits were not rerun to check reproducibility.
- **One DTE (31)**, one fold table, and one universe snapshot.
- **Selection on one 30-date warm-up fold** that precedes all option-trade
  data, so the option partitions were chosen blind.
- **Median-imputed features** (owner ruling 2026-10-02). The option features
  are imputed for 100% of warm-up rows and 82–100% of each scored fold's
  training window.
- **15 listings never scored**, and 21 tickers are scored on partial histories.
  Equal-ticker weighting gives those 21 the same weight as full-history names.
- **LightGBM used the constant Hessian** (first-order boosting). The exact
  diagonal that ADR-0236 built was not run.
- **Cache reuse depends on the store.** The cache key includes a content token
  over all of `/home/russell/data/index_options/ob`. The data config names
  `cboe-index-wide` as `iv_source`, and that source's daily snapshot lands in
  this root. Any stage run after the next snapshot rebuilds the panel (about
  631 s).
- **Unpushed and unmerged.** Branch `claude/model-zoo-417` is 15 commits ahead
  of `origin/main` (`f9f2f905`). Every artifact cited here lives in this
  worktree's Git-ignored `pipeline_runs/`, and no result is onboarded.
- The bootstrap p-values and 99.5% intervals are my recomputation from the
  run's scores, not a pipeline artifact.

## Question

After ADR-0272 (the lagged-spread Z explains the ATM pair-spread change, next-session-label R²oos +0.20 at 20 sessions) and ADR-0273 (ten feature and estimator additions, none passing the pre-registered rule; term slope the one candidate; vendor-Greek positioning worse), what model families or problem reformulations have a credible chance of forecasting the AAPL/AMZN/GOOGL relative-IV changes better, and what does each need from dskit and the Theta store? Prediction only: nothing here is a trade rule.

## Finding

**What the evidence says about the ceiling.** Implied-volatility changes are predictable and have been since the first studies; the predictability rarely survives option transaction costs. Harvey and Whaley (1992) rejected unpredictability of S&P 100 implied volatility changes and found no abnormal returns after costs; Gonçalves and Guidolin (2006) fitted a VAR to the coefficients of the S&P 500 surface, found statistically predictable dynamics, and saw most delta-hedged profit vanish as costs rose. The v6 replay and ADR-0272 reproduce that pattern on three names: a forecastable spread and an unprofitable book. "Better prediction" therefore has a realistic target (higher R²oos, better-calibrated distributions, fewer false signals), not a promise of capture.

**Why our setting is hard and what it implies.** Three names give three pairs and about 1,500 training rows per pair per year of labels; every method that added parameters (boosting, Lasso, twelve positioning columns) lost to a two-feature Ridge, and the one plausible gain (term slope) is not separable from noise with five annual folds. The binding constraint is breadth, not features. The literature that finds machine-learning gains for implied volatility does so with a wide cross-section (Kim and Oh 2026 on the whole S&P 500 surface; Almeida, Fan, Freire and Tang 2023 correcting a parametric model over the option panel; the Erasmus panel theses over the 50 most liquid U.S. stocks or 25 years of monthly equity options). Two of the ten ADR-0273 methods (pooled Ridge, realized-minus-implied) are weak three-pair versions of effects documented across hundreds of names (Goyal and Saretto 2009 is a cross-sectional sort). The Theta store already holds 414 tested names and `ChainPanelBuild` already lays out per-date surfaces for the whole universe.

**Ranked candidates** (each is a separate pre-registration on the next-session label with the ADR-0272 folds; cost and dskit status noted).

1. **Universe-pooled spread model with partial pooling, evaluated on our three pairs.** Fit the mean-reversion model (Z, change, term-slope difference, optionally leg Z) on every liquid pair of the tested universe within a sector or liquidity tercile, with pair fixed effects or hierarchical shrinkage of the reversion coefficient toward a group mean, and score only AAPL/AMZN/GOOGL pairs. Forecast-combination and Bayesian panel methods were best in a recent panel-forecasting comparison; hierarchical AR panels pool short noisy series toward a shared distribution. This is the single most likely source of a real improvement because it attacks the sample-size problem directly and tests whether the term-slope candidate generalizes. Needs: a universe surface panel (`ChainPanelBuild` + `ChainDayFeatures`, one long build), a pair-enumeration step over the panel (`PairPanelFeatures` already accepts any symbol list; a `pairs` cap and sector grouping would be new params), a pooled refit with an entity one-hot (`SklearnExpandingRefit` has no entity encoding; `CategoricalEmbeddingMLPRegressor` and `CategoricalSequenceRidgeRegressor` in `torch_ts`/`sklearn_ts` do, by one-hot), and a hierarchical Ridge (per-group intercept and slope shrinkage) as a small new estimator. Cost: a day of compute for the panel, a day of code.

2. **State-space spread: Kalman-filtered level and mean-reversion speed (Elliott, van der Hoek and Malcolm 2005).** Replace the fixed 60-session mean and standard deviation with a filtered latent level, an estimated speed κ and an innovation variance, refit online; the forecast of the h-session change is the OU expectation. It is the textbook model for this exact object and gives a model-implied horizon (half-life) instead of a fixed 60-session window. A 2024 comparison found a plain OU strategy did not beat a naive rolling Z on risk-adjusted returns, so the honest prior is "same or slightly better R²oos, better-calibrated horizon". Needs: a generic `KalmanSpreadState` node (stdlib or numpy), about a day. Cheap and clean; a natural second registration.

3. **Vol-scaled label and distributional scoring.** Spread changes are heteroskedastic; a label divided by the trailing spread standard deviation (train-time, prior-only) usually tightens linear fits, and a quantile or mixture head scored by CRPS and PIT says whether the 20-session reversal is *reliable*, which a point R² cannot. dskit has the whole stack (`horizon_cdf`, `panel_torch` heads, `predictive_cdf` curves, `distribution_scores`); the label scaling is one `ComputeFields` expression. Cost: hours. Not a new signal, a better measurement of the one we have.

4. **Per-name surface-factor dynamics, then difference.** Model each name's surface as level, slope and curvature (the Nelson–Siegel-style and VAR tradition of Chen, Han and Niu 2018 and Gonçalves and Guidolin 2006), forecast the factors jointly across the three names and QQQ, and derive the pair-spread forecast from the per-name forecasts. This uses the term and skew information as a system rather than as pair differences bolted onto a Z. Needs: a small VAR or multi-output Ridge node with chronological refits (`SklearnExpandingRefit` is single-output; `sklearn.linear_model.Ridge` is natively multi-output, so a `label` list is the extension). Cost: a day.

5. **Positioning done properly: strike-level gamma profile and signed flow.** The net OI-gamma sum we built is the crudest positioning measure; the literature that finds effects uses the gamma profile around spot and a dealer-sign convention (dealers long customer calls, short customer puts), and signed order flow rather than volume. The Theta store has strike-level OI and vendor gamma, so a near-spot gamma density per name (±5% strikes, by expiry) is one more aggregation in `ChainDayPositioning`; signed flow is not available from EOD data. Expectation: low for 5–20 session IV spreads; the published effects are intraday or next-day index returns, and the dealer-sign assumption is untested here.

6. **Tree ensembles and sequence models only with breadth.** Boosted trees beat the structural benchmark on the S&P surface in Kim and Oh (2026) with tens of thousands of surface points per day; on our 1,500 rows per pair they lost. GRU/TFT (`torch_ts`), the NeuralForecast adapter and frozen Kronos states are already in dskit and should be tried only after candidate 1 provides the panel.

**What not to do.** Re-tune on 2021–2025; add more pair-level features to the three-pair design (ADR-0273 is the evidence that this axis is exhausted); treat any of the above as a trading signal before a separately registered cost-aware study.

**Suggested order.** 3 (hours, sharpens the yardstick), then 2 (cheap, clean model of the object), then 1 (the real test, needs the universe panel), with 4 folded into 1's panel if the term-slope candidate survives, and 5 and 6 only if 1 shows the panel has signal.
